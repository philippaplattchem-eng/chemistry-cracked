Ionic Bonding

Edexcel GCSE Chemistry  ·  Topic 1  ·  Paper 1  ·  Spec 1.21, 1.25, 1.27, 1.33, 1.34

1. Key Facts

What is an ionic bond?

  • A strong electrostatic force of attraction between oppositely charged ions
  • Forms between a metal and a non-metal
  • Electrons are transferred (not shared)

Cations and Anions

  • Cation = positive ion → metal loses electrons
  • Anion = negative ion → non-metal gains electrons
  • Both reach a full outer shell (noble gas config)

2. Electronic Configurations of Ions

Atom Atom config Electrons lost / gained Ion formed Ion config (brackets & charge)
Sodium (Na) 2,8,1 Loses 1 Na⁺ [2,8]⁺
Magnesium (Mg) 2,8,2 Loses 2 Mg²⁺ [2,8]²⁺
Calcium (Ca) 2,8,8,2 Loses 2 Ca²⁺ [2,8,8]²⁺
Oxygen (O) 2,6 Gains 2 O²⁻ [2,8]²⁻
Chlorine (Cl) 2,8,7 Gains 1 Cl⁻ [2,8,8]⁻
Fluorine (F) 2,7 Gains 1 F⁻ [2,8]⁻
Pattern: The ion config is always the same as the nearest noble gas — Na⁺ and Mg²⁺ and F⁻ and O²⁻ all have [2,8], just like neon. The brackets show it is an ion; the charge in the top-right shows how many electrons were lost or gained.

3. How an Ionic Bond Forms — the 3 mark points

StepWhat happensExample (NaCl)
1 Electron(s) transferred from metal to non-metal Na loses 1e⁻ → Cl gains 1e⁻
2 Oppositely charged ions form (cation + anion) Na⁺ and Cl⁻ formed
3 Strong electrostatic force of attraction between the oppositely charged ions Na⁺ attracted to Cl⁻ → ionic bond

NaCl  Na (2,8,1) + Cl (2,8,7)

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
Charges +1 and −1 → ratio 1:1 → NaCl

MgO  Mg (2,8,2) + O (2,6)

Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻
O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻
Charges +2 and −2 → ratio 1:1 → MgO

CaF₂  Ca (2,8,8,2) + F (2,7)

Ca → Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻  ·  Each F + e⁻ → F⁻  ·  Ca²⁺ needs two F⁻ ions → CaF₂

4. Dot and Cross Diagrams — Rules

  • Show outer shell electrons only
  • Dots for one element, crosses for the other
  • Metal ion → empty outer shell (Mg loses 2 electrons → [Mg]²⁺)
  • Non-metal ion → full outer shell of 8 (O gains 2 electrons → [O]²⁻)
  • Each ion in its own square brackets with charge in top-right: [Mg]²⁺   [O]²⁻
  • If you need more than one ion (e.g. CaF₂), draw each separately with its own brackets
Never draw a shared pair between a metal and non-metal — that is covalent bonding. Always show transfer.
MgO — Mg²⁺ [2,8]²⁺ and O²⁻ [2,8]²⁻

5. Ionic Lattice Structure

  • Giant, regular, 3D arrangement of ions
  • Positive and negative ions alternate throughout
  • Held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions
  • Forces act in all directions
  • Ion ratio matches the chemical formula
Must use the word "lattice" — examiners specifically mark for it. Also state that ions alternate.
NaCl lattice — Na⁺ and Cl⁻ alternate in all directions

6. Properties of Ionic Compounds

PropertyWhat?Why?
Melting / boiling point HIGH Large amount of energy needed to overcome the many strong electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged ions in the lattice
Conducts as a solid No Ions are in fixed positions — they cannot move to carry charge
Conducts when molten Yes Lattice has broken apart — ions are free to move and carry charge
Conducts in solution Yes Ions are separated by water and are free to move and carry charge
Never say: "electrons carry the charge in molten NaCl" — the charge carriers are ions, not electrons.
Never say: "ions are free" — you must say "free to move".
Higher — comparing melting points: MgO has a higher melting point than NaCl because Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have higher charges → stronger electrostatic forces → more energy needed to break the lattice. H

7. Ionic vs Simple Molecular

PropertyIonic (e.g. NaCl)Simple molecular (e.g. CO₂)
Structure Giant lattice of ions Small separate molecules
Forces overcome on melting Strong electrostatic attractions between ions Weak intermolecular forces between molecules
Melting / boiling point High Low
Conducts when molten? Yes — ions free to move No — molecules have no charge
Classic error (Higher): "CO₂ has a low boiling point because it has weak covalent bonds." — WRONG.
The covalent bonds inside CO₂ are strong and are NOT broken. Only the weak intermolecular forces between molecules are overcome.

Rule: cation first, anion second.  ·  Transition metals need a Roman numeral: iron(II) chloride, iron(III) chloride.  ·  Brackets when anion repeats: Ca(NO₃)₂ not CaNO₃₂.

9. Exam Questions with Model Answers

Explain how an ionic bond forms between sodium and chlorine. [3 marks]
Model Answer
  • One electron is transferred from the sodium atom to the chlorine atom (1)
  • This forms a Na⁺ ion and a Cl⁻ ion (oppositely charged ions) (1)
  • There is a strong electrostatic force of attraction between the oppositely charged ions (1)
Describe the structure of solid sodium chloride. [2 marks]
Model Answer
  • Sodium chloride has a giant regular 3D lattice of alternating Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions (1)
  • The ions are held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction between the oppositely charged ions (1)
Explain why sodium chloride does not conduct electricity as a solid, but does conduct when dissolved in water. [4 marks]
Model Answer
  • As a solid, the ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice (1)
  • The ions cannot move to carry charge (1)
  • When dissolved, the lattice breaks apart and ions are separated by water molecules (1)
  • The ions are free to move through the solution and carry charge (1)
Explain why magnesium oxide has a higher melting point than sodium chloride. [3 marks] H
Model Answer
  • Both have giant ionic lattices held together by strong electrostatic forces (1)
  • Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have higher charges than Na⁺ and Cl⁻ (1)
  • This means stronger electrostatic forces, so more energy is needed to break the lattice → higher melting point (1)
Explain why carbon dioxide has a low boiling point. [2 marks] H
Model Answer
  • CO₂ consists of small molecules with weak intermolecular forces between them (1)
  • Only a small amount of energy is needed to overcome these forces (1)
Do NOT write "weak covalent bonds break" — the covalent bonds inside CO₂ are NOT broken when it boils.
