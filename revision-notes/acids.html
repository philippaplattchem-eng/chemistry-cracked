Acids, Bases & Salts

Foundation + Higher Spec 3.1–3.13 1CH0 + 1SC0 CP3 & CP6

Definitions

TermDefinition
AcidReleases H+ ions when dissolved in water; pH < 7
AlkaliSoluble base; releases OH− ions in water; pH > 7
BaseAny substance that neutralises an acid (not all bases dissolve)
NeutralisationH+(aq) + OH−(aq) → H2O(l)
SaltIonic compound formed when an acid reacts with a base
Always check hazard symbols before handling acids

The pH Scale

pH scale: 0–14 — lower = more acidic, higher = more alkaline
pHSolution typeExample
0–3Strongly acidicHCl, stomach acid (pH 2)
4–6Weakly acidicRainwater, vinegar
7NeutralPure water, NaCl solution
8–10Weakly alkalineBlood, baking soda
11–14Strongly alkalineNaOH solution, bleach
Exam tip: Measure pH accurately with a pH probe/meter. Estimate pH with universal indicator. Litmus paper only tells you acid or alkali — it does NOT give a pH value.

Universal indicator: red → orange → yellow → green (neutral) → blue → purple. Do not use it for titrations — the colour change is too gradual; use phenolphthalein or methyl orange instead.

Making Salts — Which Method?

MethodUse when…Example salt
Excess solid + filtrationBase is insoluble (can be filtered off)Copper sulfate (CuO + H2SO4)
TitrationBoth reactants are solubleSodium chloride (NaOH + HCl)
PrecipitationThe salt is insolubleLead iodide (Pb(NO3)2 + KI)

Excess Solid Method (CP3 — Copper Sulfate)

  1. Add excess copper oxide to warm dilute sulfuric acid
  2. Stir until no more solid dissolves (all acid used up)
  3. Filter to remove excess copper oxide
  4. Evaporate filtrate gently to concentrate
  5. Leave to crystallise, then filter and dry
Why excess solid? Ensures all the acid reacts — no acid left to contaminate crystals. Excess base is easily filtered off because it is insoluble.

Titration Method (CP6 — Sodium Chloride)

  1. Pipette 25.0 cm3 of alkali into a conical flask
  2. Add a few drops of indicator
  3. Fill burette with acid; record initial reading
  4. Add acid slowly; swirl; add drop by drop near end-point
  5. Stop at permanent colour change
  6. Calculate titre = final − initial reading
  7. Repeat to get concordant results (within 0.10 cm3)
  8. Calculate mean using concordant results only (2 d.p.)
Titration curve — steep section = equivalence point
Common mistakes: Including the rough titre in the mean; giving the mean to 1 d.p.; using non-concordant results; not naming which indicator you used.

Precipitation Method (Insoluble Salts)

1 — Mix two soluble salt solutions
2 — Filter to collect the precipitate
3 — Wash with distilled water
4 — Dry in oven at 60°C
Exam tip: The washing step is a required mark point — "wash with distilled water to remove soluble impurities." Don't skip it in 6-mark answers.

Strong & Weak Acids Higher

Strong acidWeak acid
ExamplesHCl, H2SO4, HNO3CH3COOH, citric acid
IonisationComplete (→)Partial (⇌)
pH (same conc.)LowerHigher
ConductivityHigherLower

Concentration = mol dm−3 (independent of strength)
Key distinction: Strong = fully ionised. Concentrated = high mol/dm3. These are independent — you can have a dilute strong acid or a concentrated weak acid. Diluting a strong acid 10× raises pH by exactly 1 unit.
