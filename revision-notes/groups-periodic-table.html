Groups in the Periodic Table

Group 1: Alkali Metals

Physical Properties

ElementSymbolAppearanceMelting Point
LithiumLiSilver metal181 °C

Group 1 properties:
➤ All are soft metals (can be cut with a knife)
➤ Low densities — Li, Na, K all float on water
➤ Stored under oil to prevent reaction with air/water
➤ Melting points decrease going down the group

Reaction with Water

General equation:
2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂
(M = alkali metal; MOH = metal hydroxide, an alkali)
MetalObservations
LiFizzes steadily, moves around, dissolves
NaFizzes vigorously, moves rapidly, melts into a ball
KFizzes very vigorously, ignites — lilac/violet flame
Memory tip: In the observation, say "fizzes" not "bubbles" — this tells the examiner you know hydrogen gas is being produced.

Reactivity Trend in Group 1

Reactivity INCREASES going down Group 1 (Li < Na < K)
[H] Explain why reactivity increases down Group 1:
  • Going down the group, each element has more electron shells
  • The outer electron is further from the nucleus
  • There is greater shielding by inner electrons
  • The outer electron is less strongly attracted to the nucleus
  • It is more easily lost — so the metal is more reactive
Key phrase: "outer electron is further from the nucleus and more easily lost"
Outer electron moves further from nucleus
down Group 1 → easier to lose → more reactive

Group 7: Halogens

Physical Properties

Trends down Group 7:
➤ Melting and boiling points increase
➤ Reactivity decreases (opposite to Group 1!)
➤ All exist as diatomic molecules (Cl₂, Br₂, I₂)
➤ All are non-metals

Displacement Reactions

A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halide from solution.
Halogen addedKCl solutionKBr solutionKI solution
Cl₂ No reaction Orange — Br₂ formed Brown — I₂ formed
Br₂ No reaction No reaction Brown — I₂ formed
I₂ No reaction No reaction No reaction
e.g. Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂ (Cl is more reactive than Br, so it displaces Br)
Memory tip: Chlorine beats Bromine beats Iodine (C before B before I = "Can Butterflies Ignore" reactivity order).
[H] Explain why reactivity decreases down Group 7:
  • Going down the group, each element has more electron shells
  • The outer shell is further from the nucleus and has more shielding by inner electrons
  • The nucleus has a weaker attraction for incoming electrons
  • It is harder to gain an electron — so the halogen is less reactive
Contrast with Group 1: Group 1 metals lose electrons → more shells = easier to lose = more reactive. Group 7 non-metals gain electrons → more shells = harder to gain = less reactive.
More shells down Group 7 → outer shell further from nucleus → harder to gain e⁻ → less reactive

Group 0: Noble Gases

ElementConfigBoiling PointUse
Helium (He)2−269 °CBalloons, airships (safer than H₂)
Neon (Ne)2.8−246 °CAdvertising signs (glows red-orange)
Argon (Ar)2.8.8−186 °CFilling light bulbs & welding (inert shield)
Why are noble gases unreactive?
They have a full outer electron shell — no need to gain or lose electrons to become stable. They do not form bonds under normal conditions.
⚠ He has a full outer shell of 2 electrons — its first shell is the only shell. Ne and Ar both have 8 in their outer shell.
Trend in boiling points: Boiling points increase going down Group 0. (He has the lowest b.p. of any element: −269 °C)
Noble gas electron configurations — full outer shells
Common error: Writing that noble gases have "8 outer electrons" for all of them — He has only 2 electrons total (its first shell IS the outer shell). Always treat He as a special case with a full outer shell of 2. (Spec 6.3.1.1)

Exam technique (Nov 2022): When asked to "explain" a reactivity trend, always state three steps: (1) more/fewer shells, (2) outer electron/shell further/closer to nucleus, (3) weaker/stronger attraction → more/less easily lost/gained. One-sentence answers score 1/3 marks maximum.
