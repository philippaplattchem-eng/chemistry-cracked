Methods of Separating Mixtures

Foundation + Higher Spec 2.20–2.26 CP1 Chromatography

Filtration

Filtration

Filtration

  • Separates: insoluble solid from a liquid
  • The liquid + dissolved substances pass through the filter paper as the filtrate
  • The insoluble solid is left behind on the filter paper as the residue
  • Works because the solid particles are too large to pass through the tiny holes in the paper
Exam tip: State what is in the residue AND what is in the filtrate in your answers. "The sand is left in the filter paper as residue; the salt solution passes through as filtrate."

Crystallisation

Crystallisation

Crystallisation

  • Separates: a dissolved solid from its solution (gives pure crystals)
  • Heat the solution in an evaporating basin to drive off most of the water
  • Stop heating when you see the first crystals forming at the edges (or reduce volume to ~⅓)
  • Allow to cool slowly — large, well-formed crystals grow as solubility decreases
  • Filter off the crystals and pat dry between filter paper
Never evaporate to complete dryness! Heating crystals can cause them to decompose or shatter. Stop when you see crystallisation beginning at the edges of the liquid.

Simple Distillation

Simple Distillation

Simple Distillation

  • Separates: a solvent from a solution (e.g. pure water from salt water)
  • Heat the solution — the solvent evaporates, leaving the solute behind
  • Vapour passes through the condenser where it cools and condenses back to liquid
  • Pure solvent is collected in the receiving flask
Key exam point — Thermometer position: The thermometer bulb must be placed at the side-arm exit (not in the liquid). This measures the temperature of the vapour leaving — when it matches the solvent's boiling point, you know the pure vapour is condensing.
Limitation: If two liquids have similar boiling points, simple distillation cannot separate them — use fractional distillation instead.

Fractional Distillation

Fractional Distillation

Fractional Distillation

  • Separates: two or more miscible liquids with different boiling points (e.g. ethanol + water, crude oil fractions)
  • The mixture is heated — the most volatile liquid (lowest b.p.) evaporates first
  • The fractionating column maintains a temperature gradient (hot at bottom, cooler at top)
  • Vapours with a higher b.p. condense on the glass beads and drip back down
  • Only the vapour with the lowest b.p. reaches the top and enters the condenser first
  • Collect fractions in separate containers as temperature increases
Difference from simple distillation: The fractionating column (packed with glass beads) allows repeated condensation/evaporation cycles — this gives much better separation of liquids with similar b.p.s.

Paper Chromatography

Paper Chromatography (CP1)

Paper Chromatography

  • Separates: mixtures of dyes, pigments, amino acids, food colourings
  • Stationary phase: the paper (water adsorbed on paper fibres)
  • Mobile phase: the solvent (moves up the paper by capillary action)
  • Draw the baseline in pencil (not pen — pen would dissolve in the solvent)
  • The baseline must be above the solvent level in the beaker at all times
  • Substances more soluble in the mobile phase travel further up the paper
  • A pure substance gives only one spot; a mixture gives multiple spots
[H] Rf (Retardation Factor) — Higher Tier Only
Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent front
Rf has no units (it is a ratio)  |  Rf is always between 0 and 1
Example: Spot moves 3.0 cm; solvent front moves 8.0 cm → Rf = 3.0 ÷ 8.0 = 0.375
Rf values can be used to identify unknown substances by comparing with known values from a data table.
Common error: Measuring from the top of the paper instead of from the pencil baseline. Always measure from the baseline.

Paper 1 exam strategy: Questions on separating mixtures often ask you to "explain" your choice of method — always state BOTH what you are separating AND the property that makes the method work (e.g., "I would use fractional distillation because ethanol and water are miscible liquids with different boiling points, so they can be separated by their different volatilities in the column").
